The difference

We do not run a departure you join. We run a journey that belongs to you.

Most Egypt programmes at this level are group departures on fixed dates, sold to between six and eighteen travellers who meet at the airport. That model exists for a reason, and it works well for people who want it.

This is not that. Every journey here is arranged for a single party, on the dates that suit them, with one licensed Egyptologist and one coordinator from the first email to the morning they fly home.

A private itinerary can be changed on the morning of. A group departure of eighteen cannot.

01  The journeys

Four shapes, and none of them fixed.

What follows are the routes we run most often. They are starting points rather than products. Nearly every journey we operate began as one of these and ended up somewhere slightly different once we knew who was travelling and why.

Seven days

Giza, Luxor and Aswan by internal flight. The shortest route that does not feel rushed.

Nine days

The river journey. Giza, then Aswan down to Luxor by boat. No Red Sea.

Ten days

The widest. Adds Alexandria and the Mediterranean coast to the classic route.

Fourteen days

Upper Egypt in full, with Abydos, Dendera and the Western Desert.

Lengths are indicative. We have run eleven days and we have run twenty two. Any of these can end with a few days on the Red Sea at Hurghada, or carry on across the border into Jordan as a single journey. The question is never how long the brochure says, it is how long you have.

02  The river, privately

A boat on the Nile with nobody else aboard.

A dahabiya is the older way of travelling the Nile: a small sailing boat, moved by the wind where the wind allows, that moors at sandbanks and villages the large ships pass without stopping.

When we put a party on a dahabiya, the boat is theirs. The crew, the deck and the table are not shared with another family, and the day is shaped around the people on board rather than a printed timetable.

The cruise ship

Fixed weekly departures, a larger ship, and a full programme of amenities. The right choice when the dates line up and a party wants a hotel that happens to float.

The dahabiya

Your dates, your pace, and a stop wherever the afternoon suggests one. The right choice when the river itself is the reason for coming.

On a dahabiya, the question at breakfast is not what time the boat leaves. It is where you would like it to go.

Which of the two suits a journey is settled in the first conversation, before anything is booked. We sail both, so we have no reason to prefer one.

03  Before the gates open

Some temples are best seen when nobody else is in them.

For selected temples we arrange private access outside public hours, either before the gates open in the morning or after they close in the evening. The site is the same. What changes is that the only voices in it are yours and your guide's.

These visits are not a ticket bought at the door. Each one is requested in advance, for a named site on a named date, and is only placed in your itinerary once it has been confirmed.

The same hall, with nobody else in it, is a different building.

How it fits the day

A morning visit means an early start and a slow breakfast afterwards. An evening visit usually replaces the hottest part of the afternoon, which is spent at the hotel or on the water instead.

Private access depends on the site, the season and the authorities on the day. We never promise it until it is confirmed, and if it cannot be arranged we say so before you travel rather than after you arrive.

05  How a journey is run

One guide. One coordinator. One number.

Your Egyptologist

A licensed guide who stays with you for the whole journey rather than a different face at every site.

There is no handover between cities and no new guide learning your names on day four.

Your coordinator

One person writes your itinerary and stays with it from the first email to the morning you fly home.

Nothing is handed to a call centre, and you are never asked to explain your own trip to somebody who has not read it.

Reachable at any hour

A flight moves, a site closes, somebody wakes up unwell, and the plan has to change before breakfast.

Write or call at any hour and the answer comes from the office in Cairo that built the itinerary, not from a duty desk in another time zone.

Operated, not brokered

We are based in Cairo and deal with the hotels, the cruise and the guides directly.

When something needs fixing, it is fixed by the people who arranged it in the first place.

07  Money, plainly

No figure in this document, and no surprises in the proposal.

Journeys are costed individually, because a nine day itinerary for two people in February and the same itinerary in August are not the same piece of work. Every proposal we send carries one total, what it covers, and what it does not.

Gratuities

Tips for your guide, your drivers and the cruise staff are included in the journey cost and settled by us. You are not expected to carry envelopes, and nobody on our side will ask you for anything.

Restaurant and hotel staff outside the itinerary are at your discretion, as anywhere.

Currency

Proposals are quoted in US dollars. Small cash in Egyptian pounds is useful for a market or a coffee and nothing else.

Payment schedule, deposit and cancellation terms are issued in writing with each proposal, before anything is paid. We do not publish a single policy here because the terms follow the hotels and the cruise on your particular dates.

One number reaches the person who built your itinerary, at any hour, for the whole trip.

We are in Cairo, in the same country as you, which is the part that matters at three in the morning.

09  When to travel

Egypt has a season, and it is not a secret.

Oct to AprThe comfortable months, and the busy ones. Upper Egypt is warm in the day and cool at night. Book early, particularly for Christmas and Easter.
MayStill workable, with rising heat in Luxor and Aswan by the afternoon. Quieter at every site.
Jun to AugHot in Upper Egypt, genuinely so. It can be done, and we do it, by moving the day earlier and the afternoons indoors or onto the water. The sites are at their emptiest and the river is at its best.
SepThe heat begins to ease toward the end of the month. A good time for Cairo, Alexandria and the coast, and an early start on the season in the south before it fills.

Ramadan

The month moves about eleven days earlier each year. Sites stay open, some opening hours shorten, and the evenings in Cairo are at their liveliest. If your dates fall inside it, we tell you in the proposal and plan the days around it.

The best month is the one you can travel in. What changes with the season is how the day is shaped, not whether the journey is worth making.

11  Health and entry

The practical part, kept short.

Passport and visa

US passport holders need a tourist visa. It can be arranged online before you fly or on arrival at the airport, and our representative is with you for the arrival route.

Your passport should be valid for at least six months beyond your arrival date, with a blank page for the visa.

Vaccinations and medication

Your own doctor is the right person to advise on routine vaccinations before you travel.

Carry prescription medication in its original packaging, with a copy of the prescription, and enough for the whole trip.

Water and food

Drink bottled water throughout, which is provided in every vehicle. Your guide knows which kitchens to trust and which to walk past.

Insurance

Travel insurance is not included and we ask every guest to hold it, with cover for medical care and evacuation.

Entry rules and fees change without much notice. We confirm the current requirements with every proposal rather than print them here.

12  Questions we are asked

The ones that come up most.

Can the itinerary change once we are travelling?

Yes. That is most of the point of travelling privately. Hotels, the cruise and any private access set some limits, and everything else moves with you.

Is Egypt safe for American travellers?

We follow the US State Department advisory closely and plan routes within it. Every transfer is private, and your guide is with you at every site.

Can we travel with children?

Yes. The days are shorter, the pace is slower, and the guide is chosen for families rather than for scholars.

Can Jordan be part of the same journey?

Yes. Egypt and Jordan are often combined into one trip, planned and run by the same coordinator.

We have a travel advisor. Can they book with you?

Yes. We work with advisors directly, and they remain your point of contact throughout.

How far ahead should we book?

For October to April, as early as you can, particularly around the holidays. Summer journeys can often be arranged at shorter notice.
